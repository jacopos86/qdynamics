This note derives only the metric-regularized Schur response used to nominate a generator deletion in Paper I SNAKE. The object is a ranking surrogate; the measured delete/refit energy test remains the acceptance authority.

\section*{Executive Reading}

The implemented prune route is best read as a local blocked quadratic elimination problem. Deleting coordinate \(j\) forces \(\Delta\theta_j=-\theta_j\); the survivor set \(D\) then tries to compensate through \(\delta\theta_D\), and the nomination score measures how much of the frozen deletion burden is recovered by that local survivor response.

The implementation forms the metric-regularized block \(\mathcal Q=H+\eta G\) before blocking the deleted coordinate from the survivors. Thus \(H\) is the physical energy Hessian block at the current refit ansatz, \(G\) is the real horizontally projected Fubini--Study Gram block on the ansatz tangents, and \(\mathcal Q_{Dj}=H_{Dj}+\eta G_{Dj}\) enters the right-hand side of the survivor solve. This is the full deleted-coordinate recoverability surrogate. It is not the response-only damping model in which \(G\) appears only as \(G_{DD}\) on the left-hand side.

Two audit points are the useful mental guardrails. First, the scalar \(\Lambda(A_j)\) is a metric-regularized Schur nomination surrogate, because the score is computed from \(\mathcal Q\), not from \(H\) alone. Second, the Schur-complement language is exact only for an invertible survivor block; with a pseudoinverse or ridge it is a generalized or regularized Schur-reduced burden. The measured delete/refit branch remains the object that decides whether deletion is actually accepted.

The algorithmic chain is: forced deletion, survivor-response quadratic, stationary blocked solve, Schur-reduced frozen-minus-credit score, nomination, measured delete/refit acceptance. The geometric chain is: horizontal projection, projected tangent Gram \(G\), deleted-tangent residual, metric regularization, full recoverability surrogate. These chains coincide only in the metric-only least-squares projection problem; in the implemented prune route they are coupled through \(\mathcal Q=H+\eta G\).


\subsection*{Paper-I Notation Bridge}

The manuscript writes the metric-regularized curvature as
\[
\mathcal Q:=H+\eta G,
\]
and denotes the deletion score and removed-cost normalization by
\[
\Lambda(A_j),
\qquad
\frac{\Lambda(A_j)}{K_j}.
\]
Those symbols are used throughout this note. The index \(j\) fixes the generator proposed for deletion, so the survivor set is written simply as \(D=\{1,\ldots,n_k\}\setminus\{j\}\), rather than carrying a redundant \(D_j\) subscript through every block. Likewise, the manuscript evaluates the construction at the optimized point \(\boldsymbol\theta_k^\star\). To keep the component derivation readable, the note suppresses the star after this declaration: every \(\theta_i\), \(H\), \(G\), and \(\mathcal Q\) below is evaluated at \(\boldsymbol\theta_k^\star\) unless a trial displacement is shown explicitly.

\section*{Local Deletion Geometry}

\micro{Deleting coordinate \(j\) fixes one parameter displacement and asks the surviving coordinates to compensate it.}

\deriv
At adaptive step \(k\), write the current refit ansatz and state as
\[
\begin{aligned}
U_k(\boldsymbol\theta_k)
&=
\prod_{i=1}^{n_k}\exp(\theta_iA_i),
&
\mathbf A_k&=(A_1,\ldots,A_{n_k}),
\\
\ket{\psi_k}
&=
\ket{\psi(U_k,\boldsymbol\theta_k)}\in\mathcal H,
&
\boldsymbol\theta_k&=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_{n_k})^{\mathsf T}\in\mathbb R^{n_k}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(j\in\{1,\ldots,n_k\}\), define
\[
D:=\{1,\ldots,n_k\}\setminus\{j\},
\qquad
d:=|D|=n_k-1.
\]
Writing the inherited increasing order of \(D\) as
\[
D=(d_\alpha)_{\alpha=1}^{d},
\qquad
1\le d_1<\cdots<d_d\le n_k ,
\]
the deleted and survivor parameters obey
\[
\theta_j'=0,
\qquad
\theta_{d_\alpha}'=\theta_{d_\alpha}+\delta\theta_{d_\alpha},
\qquad
1\le\alpha\le d.
\]
The deletion displacement and survivor response are
\[
-\theta_j\in\mathbb R,
\qquad
\delta\theta_D=(\delta\theta_u)_{u\in D}\in\mathbb R^d.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\Delta\theta_a
=
-\theta_j\,\mathbf 1\{a=j\}
+
\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}\delta\theta_{d_\alpha}\mathbf 1\{a=d_\alpha\},
\qquad
1\le a\le n_k .
\]
The combined displacement over \(\{j\}\cup D\) is
\[
x_j(\delta\theta_D)
=
\begin{bmatrix}
-\theta_j\\
\delta\theta_D
\end{bmatrix}
\in\mathbb R^{1+d}.
\]

\phys{The ablation decision is not a small perturbation in the deleted coordinate: it deterministically removes \(\theta_j\). The local approximation asks whether the survivor generators can mimic that deletion through \(\delta\theta_D\).}

\micro{The implementation builds one symmetric quadratic block that combines energy curvature with Fubini--Study geometry.}

\deriv
For \(a,b\in\{j\}\cup D\), define
\[
\tau_a
=
\partial_{\theta_a}\ket{\psi(U_k,\boldsymbol\theta_k)}
\in\mathcal H,
\]
\[
H_{ab}
=
\partial_{\theta_a}\partial_{\theta_b}
E(U_k,\boldsymbol\theta_k)
\in\mathbb R,
\]
\[
G_{ab}
=
\operatorname{Re}\langle \Pi\tau_a,\Pi\tau_b\rangle
\in\mathbb R,
\]
with \(\Pi:\mathcal H\to\mathcal H\) the horizontal projector at the current state,
\[
\Pi=I_{\mathcal H}-\ket{\psi_k}\bra{\psi_k},
\qquad
\Pi^\dagger=\Pi,
\qquad
\Pi^2=\Pi .
\]
For real coordinates,
\[
\begin{aligned}
G_{ba}
&=
\operatorname{Re}\langle \Pi\tau_b,\Pi\tau_a\rangle
=
\operatorname{Re}\overline{\langle \Pi\tau_a,\Pi\tau_b\rangle}
=G_{ab},
\\
H_{ba}&=H_{ab}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The projected Gram block is positive semidefinite because, for \(x=(x_a)\in\mathbb R^{n_k}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
x^{\mathsf T}Gx
&=
\sum_{a,b=1}^{n_k}x_aG_{ab}x_b
\\
&=
\operatorname{Re}
\left\langle
\sum_{a=1}^{n_k}x_a\Pi\tau_a,
\sum_{b=1}^{n_k}x_b\Pi\tau_b
\right\rangle
\\
&=
\left\|
\sum_{a=1}^{n_k}x_a\Pi\tau_a
\right\|_{\mathcal H}^{2}
\ge0 .
\end{aligned}
\]
The implemented metric Schur route first forms symmetrized positive-semidefinite blocks, still denoted \(H\) and \(G\), and then sets
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Q&:=H+\eta G,
&
\eta&\ge 0,
\\
H&\succeq0,
&
G&\succeq0,
\\
\mathcal Q&=\mathcal Q^{\mathsf T}\succeq0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Restrict \(\mathcal Q\) to \(\{j\}\cup D\):
\[
\mathcal Q_{\{j\}\cup D,\{j\}\cup D}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathcal Q_{jj} & \mathcal Q_{jD}\\
\mathcal Q_{Dj} & \mathcal Q_{DD}
\end{bmatrix},
\]
\[
\mathcal Q_{Dj}=(\mathcal Q_{uj})_{u\in D}\in\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
\mathcal Q_{DD}=(\mathcal Q_{uv})_{u,v\in D}\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}.
\]
In the ordered survivor labels \(D=(d_\alpha)_{\alpha=1}^{d}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Q_{Dj}
&=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathcal Q_{d_1j}\\
\mathcal Q_{d_2j}\\
\vdots\\
\mathcal Q_{d_dj}
\end{bmatrix},
\\
\mathcal Q_{DD}
&=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathcal Q_{d_1d_1} & \mathcal Q_{d_1d_2} & \cdots & \mathcal Q_{d_1d_d}\\
\mathcal Q_{d_2d_1} & \mathcal Q_{d_2d_2} & \cdots & \mathcal Q_{d_2d_d}\\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
\mathcal Q_{d_dd_1} & \mathcal Q_{d_dd_2} & \cdots & \mathcal Q_{d_dd_d}
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(\mathcal Q=\mathcal Q^{\mathsf T}\),
\[
\mathcal Q_{jD}=\mathcal Q_{Dj}^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Expanding \(\mathcal Q=H+\eta G\) inside the deletion blocks,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Q_{jj}&=H_{jj}+\eta G_{jj},
&
\mathcal Q_{Dj}&=H_{Dj}+\eta G_{Dj},
\\
\mathcal Q_{jD}&=H_{jD}+\eta G_{jD},
&
\mathcal Q_{DD}&=H_{DD}+\eta G_{DD}.
\end{aligned}
\]

\phys{The metric contribution is a geometric nomination penalty. It adds Fubini--Study deleted-tangent recoverability to the Schur curvature model.}

\section*{Quadratic Surrogate}

\micro{The score starts from the quadratic form induced by the block \(\mathcal Q=H+\eta G\).}

\deriv
Define the local metric-Schur deletion surrogate
\[
\Phi_j^\ominus(\delta\theta_D)
:=
\frac12
x_j(\delta\theta_D)^{\mathsf T}
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathcal Q_{jj} & \mathcal Q_{jD}\\
\mathcal Q_{Dj} & \mathcal Q_{DD}
\end{bmatrix}
x_j(\delta\theta_D).
\]
Substitute \(x_j(\delta\theta_D)=(-\theta_j,\delta\theta_D)^{\mathsf T}\):
{\scriptsize
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_j^\ominus(\delta\theta_D)
&=
\frac12
\begin{bmatrix}
-\theta_j & \delta\theta_D^{\mathsf T}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathcal Q_{jj} & \mathcal Q_{jD}\\
\mathcal Q_{Dj} & \mathcal Q_{DD}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
-\theta_j\\
\delta\theta_D
\end{bmatrix}
\\
&=
\frac12
\begin{bmatrix}
-\theta_j & \delta\theta_D^{\mathsf T}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
-\theta_j\mathcal Q_{jj}+\mathcal Q_{jD}\delta\theta_D\\
-\theta_j\mathcal Q_{Dj}+\mathcal Q_{DD}\delta\theta_D
\end{bmatrix}
\\
&=
\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{jj}
-\frac12\theta_j\mathcal Q_{jD}\delta\theta_D
-\frac12\theta_j\delta\theta_D^{\mathsf T}\mathcal Q_{Dj}
+\frac12\delta\theta_D^{\mathsf T}\mathcal Q_{DD}\delta\theta_D
\\
&=
\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{jj}
-\theta_j\mathcal Q_{Dj}^{\mathsf T}\delta\theta_D
+\frac12\delta\theta_D^{\mathsf T}\mathcal Q_{DD}\delta\theta_D .
\end{aligned}
\]
}
The component form is
\[
\Phi_j^\ominus(\delta\theta_D)
=
\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{jj}
-\theta_j\sum_{u\in D}\mathcal Q_{uj}\delta\theta_u
+\frac12\sum_{u\in D}\sum_{v\in D}
\delta\theta_u\mathcal Q_{uv}\delta\theta_v.
\]
The multiplications are shape-compatible because
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Q_{jD}\delta\theta_D
&\in \mathbb R^{1\times d}\mathbb R^d=\mathbb R,
\\
\mathcal Q_{DD}\delta\theta_D
&\in \mathbb R^{d\times d}\mathbb R^d=\mathbb R^d,
\\
\mathcal Q_{Dj}^{\mathsf T}\delta\theta_D
&\in \mathbb R^{1\times d}\mathbb R^d=\mathbb R .
\end{aligned}
\]

\phys{The first term is the frozen deletion burden. The middle term is the recoverability credit from coupling the deleted coordinate to survivors. The last term is the quadratic cost of moving survivors.}

\micro{The same expression expands into energy curvature and Fubini--Study geometry by substituting \(\mathcal Q=H+\eta G\).}

\deriv
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_j^\ominus(\delta\theta_D)
={}&
\frac12\theta_j^2(H_{jj}+\eta G_{jj})
\\
&-\theta_j
(H_{Dj}+\eta G_{Dj})^{\mathsf T}\delta\theta_D
\\
&+\frac12
\delta\theta_D^{\mathsf T}
(H_{DD}+\eta G_{DD})
\delta\theta_D .
\end{aligned}
\]
The Fubini--Study part alone is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_{j,G}^\ominus(\delta\theta_D)
&=
\frac12
\left\|
\Pi\left(
-\theta_j\tau_j+\sum_{u\in D}\delta\theta_u\tau_u
\right)
\right\|^2
\\
&=
\frac12\theta_j^2G_{jj}
-\theta_jG_{Dj}^{\mathsf T}\delta\theta_D
+\frac12\delta\theta_D^{\mathsf T}G_{DD}\delta\theta_D .
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
\Phi_j^\ominus(\delta\theta_D)
=
\Phi_{j,H}^\ominus(\delta\theta_D)
+\eta\Phi_{j,G}^\ominus(\delta\theta_D).
\]

\phys{The \(G_{Dj}\) cross block appears because the implemented model penalizes the full deleted-coordinate plus survivor-response displacement. This is the deleted-tangent recoverability model.}

\section*{Schur Response}

\micro{The Schur response is the survivor displacement that minimizes the local surrogate.}

\deriv
The optimization variable and objective are
\[
\delta\theta_D\in\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
\delta\theta_D^\star
\in
\arg\min_{\delta\theta_D\in\mathbb R^d}
\Phi_j^\ominus(\delta\theta_D).
\]
The derivative shapes are
\[
\mathcal Q_{Dj}\in\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
\mathcal Q_{DD}\delta\theta_D\in\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
\nabla_{\delta\theta_D}\Phi_j^\ominus\in\mathbb R^d.
\]
Differentiate the component form:
{\small
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial \Phi_j^\ominus}{\partial \delta\theta_w}
&=
\frac{\partial}{\partial\delta\theta_w}
\left[
\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{jj}
-\theta_j\sum_{u\in D}\mathcal Q_{uj}\delta\theta_u
+\frac12\sum_{u,v\in D}
\delta\theta_u\mathcal Q_{uv}\delta\theta_v
\right]
\\
&=
-\theta_j\mathcal Q_{wj}
+\frac12\sum_{v\in D}\mathcal Q_{wv}\delta\theta_v
+\frac12\sum_{u\in D}\delta\theta_u\mathcal Q_{uw}
\\
&=
-\theta_j\mathcal Q_{wj}
+\sum_{v\in D}\mathcal Q_{wv}\delta\theta_v,
\qquad w\in D.
\end{aligned}
\]
}
In vector form,
\[
\nabla_{\delta\theta_D}\Phi_j^\ominus
=
-\theta_j\mathcal Q_{Dj}
+\mathcal Q_{DD}\delta\theta_D.
\]
Stationarity gives
\[
-\theta_j\mathcal Q_{Dj}+\mathcal Q_{DD}\delta\theta_D^\star=0,
\qquad
\mathcal Q_{DD}\delta\theta_D^\star=\theta_j\mathcal Q_{Dj}.
\]
With the pseudoinverse convention used in the manuscript display,
\[
\boxed{
\delta\theta_D^\star
=
\theta_j\mathcal Q_{DD}^{+}\mathcal Q_{Dj}
}
\]
and, after substituting \(\mathcal Q=H+\eta G\),
\[
\boxed{
\delta\theta_D^\star
=
\theta_j
\left(H_{DD}+\eta G_{DD}\right)^+
\left(H_{Dj}+\eta G_{Dj}\right).
}
\]

\phys{The survivor coordinates move in the direction that best explains the deleted coordinate under the metric-regularized curvature block. The term \(G_{Dj}\) is required because the deleted tangent participates in the implemented surrogate.}

\micro{The code uses the same normal equation with an explicit ridge block and a right-hand side.}

\deriv
The implementation-level objects are
\[
R:=\mathcal Q_{DD}+\lambda I_d\in\mathbb R^{d\times d},
\qquad
\rhs:=\theta_j\mathcal Q_{Dj}\in\mathbb R^d,
\]
\[
\delta\theta_D^\star=R^+\rhs.
\]
When \(\lambda=0\), this reduces to the displayed manuscript convention:
\[
R=\mathcal Q_{DD},
\qquad
\delta\theta_D^\star
=
\theta_j\mathcal Q_{DD}^{+}\mathcal Q_{Dj}.
\]
When the gradient-corrected route is enabled, the code changes only the right-hand side:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_{j,\mathrm{grad}}^\ominus(\delta\theta_D)
&=
g_D^{\mathsf T}\delta\theta_D
-\theta_j\mathcal Q_{Dj}^{\mathsf T}\delta\theta_D
+\frac12\delta\theta_D^{\mathsf T}\mathcal Q_{DD}\delta\theta_D,
\\
\nabla_{\delta\theta_D}\Phi_{j,\mathrm{grad}}^\ominus
&=
g_D-\theta_j\mathcal Q_{Dj}+\mathcal Q_{DD}\delta\theta_D,
\\
\mathcal Q_{DD}\delta\theta_D^\star
&=
\theta_j\mathcal Q_{Dj}-g_D .
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
\rhs_{\mathrm{grad}}
=
\theta_j\mathcal Q_{Dj}-g_D,
\qquad
\delta\theta_D^\star
=
R^+\rhs_{\mathrm{grad}}.
\]
The stationary default used for the current route sets
\[
g_D=0.
\]

\phys{The manuscript can suppress \(\lambda I_d\) in the displayed equations because the inverse-notation convention states that pseudoinverses are regularized. The implemented default is stationary, so no survivor-gradient term belongs in the main formula.}

\section*{Normal-Equation Reading}

\micro{The Fubini--Study part alone is a literal least-squares reconstruction of the deleted tangent by survivor tangents.}

\deriv
Define the survivor tangent synthesis map \(T_D:\mathbb R^d\to\mathcal H\) by
\[
T_D\delta\theta_D
:=
\sum_{u\in D}\delta\theta_u\Pi\tau_u,
\qquad
y_j:=\theta_j\Pi\tau_j\in\mathcal H .
\]
The metric-only recovery problem is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal J_G(\delta\theta_D)
&:=
\left\|
y_j-T_D\delta\theta_D
\right\|_{\mathcal H}^2,
\\
\delta_G^\star
&\in
\arg\min_{\delta\theta_D\in\mathbb R^d}
\mathcal J_G(\delta\theta_D).
\end{aligned}
\]
Expanding,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal J_G(\delta\theta_D)
&=
\theta_j^2G_{jj}
-2\theta_jG_{Dj}^{\mathsf T}\delta\theta_D
+\delta\theta_D^{\mathsf T}G_{DD}\delta\theta_D,
\\
\nabla_{\delta\theta_D}\mathcal J_G
&=
-2\theta_jG_{Dj}
+2G_{DD}\delta\theta_D .
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the metric-only normal equation is
\[
G_{DD}\delta_G^\star=\theta_jG_{Dj}.
\]

\phys{Least squares is exact for the \(G\)-only residual: survivors synthesize a projected tangent vector that approximates the deleted projected tangent. The implemented metric-Schur solve has the same normal-equation shape, but with \(G\) blended with energy curvature.}

\micro{The full route can be read as a Lagrange-style metric damping of the local energy curvature model.}

\deriv
Under the stationary convention, the Hessian-only deletion quadratic is
\[
\Phi_{j,H}^\ominus(\delta\theta_D)
=
\frac12\theta_j^2H_{jj}
-\theta_jH_{Dj}^{\mathsf T}\delta\theta_D
+\frac12\delta\theta_D^{\mathsf T}H_{DD}\delta\theta_D.
\]
A metric trust-region relaxation uses
\[
\Phi_{j,H}^\ominus(\delta\theta_D)
\quad
\text{with}
\quad
\mathcal R_j^G(\delta\theta_D)\le\rho^2 .
\]
The Lagrangian objective is
\[
\mathcal L_j(\delta\theta_D;\eta)
=
\Phi_{j,H}^\ominus(\delta\theta_D)
+
\frac{\eta}{2}
\left(
\mathcal R_j^G(\delta\theta_D)-\rho^2
\right).
\]
The constant \(-\eta\rho^2/2\) does not change the minimizer, so stationarity gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&=
-\theta_jH_{Dj}
+H_{DD}\delta\theta_D
\\
&\quad
+\eta\left(-\theta_jG_{Dj}+G_{DD}\delta\theta_D\right),
\\
(H_{DD}+\eta G_{DD})
\delta\theta_D
&=
\theta_jH_{Dj}+\eta\theta_jG_{Dj}.
\end{aligned}
\]

\phys{The parameter \(\eta\) weights geometric recoverability inside the nomination model. Because the metric penalty is applied to the full deleted-coordinate plus survivor-response displacement, the cross block \(G_{Dj}\) appears on the right-hand side.}

\section*{Nomination Score}

\micro{The optimized score is the frozen deletion burden minus the recoverability credit, clipped below at zero.}

\deriv
Using \(R=\mathcal Q_{DD}+\lambda I_d\), \(\rhs=\theta_j\mathcal Q_{Dj}\), and \(\delta=R^+\rhs\), the code-level quantities are
\[
\mathrm{frozen}
:=
\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{jj},
\]
\[
\mathrm{credit}
:=
\rhs^{\mathsf T}\delta
-\frac12\delta^{\mathsf T}R\delta,
\]
\[
\Lambda(A_j)
:=
\max\{0,\mathrm{frozen}-\mathrm{credit}\}.
\]
The scalar shapes are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rhs^{\mathsf T}\delta
&\in\mathbb R^{1\times d}\mathbb R^d=\mathbb R,
\\
\delta^{\mathsf T}R\delta
&\in
\mathbb R^{1\times d}\mathbb R^{d\times d}\mathbb R^d
=\mathbb R,
\\
\mathrm{frozen},\mathrm{credit},\Lambda(A_j)&\in\mathbb R .
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(R\delta=\rhs\), then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{credit}
&=
\rhs^{\mathsf T}\delta
-\frac12\delta^{\mathsf T}R\delta
\\
&=
\delta^{\mathsf T}R\delta
-\frac12\delta^{\mathsf T}R\delta
\\
&=
\frac12\delta^{\mathsf T}R\delta
\\
&=
\frac12\rhs^{\mathsf T}R^+\rhs .
\end{aligned}
\]
With displayed ridge suppressed, \(\rhs=\theta_j\mathcal Q_{Dj}\) and \(R=\mathcal Q_{DD}\), so
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{credit}^\star
&=
\frac12\rhs^{\mathsf T}\mathcal Q_{DD}^{+}\rhs
\\
&=
\frac12
(\theta_j\mathcal Q_{Dj})^{\mathsf T}
\mathcal Q_{DD}^{+}
(\theta_j\mathcal Q_{Dj})
\\
&=
\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{Dj}^{\mathsf T}\mathcal Q_{DD}^{+}\mathcal Q_{Dj}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\Lambda(A_j)
=
\max\left\{
0,\,
\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{jj}
-\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{Dj}^{\mathsf T}\mathcal Q_{DD}^{+}\mathcal Q_{Dj}
\right\}.
}
\]
Equivalently, the optimized scalar block is
\[
\boxed{
\Lambda(A_j)
=
\frac12\theta_j^2
\max\left\{
0,\,
\mathcal Q_{jj}-\mathcal Q_{jD}\mathcal Q_{DD}^{+}\mathcal Q_{Dj}
\right\}.
}
\]
If \(\mathcal Q_{DD}\) is invertible, then
\[
S_{j\mid D}
:=
\mathcal Q_{jj}-\mathcal Q_{jD}\mathcal Q_{DD}^{-1}\mathcal Q_{Dj}
\]
is the scalar Schur complement of \(\mathcal Q_{DD}\) in the block on \(\{j\}\cup D\). With a pseudoinverse or explicit ridge, the same phrase means the regularized Schur-reduced burden used for nomination:
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{j\mid D}^{+}
&:=
\mathcal Q_{jj}-\mathcal Q_{jD}\mathcal Q_{DD}^{+}\mathcal Q_{Dj},
\\
\Lambda(A_j)
&=
\frac12\theta_j^2\max\{0,S_{j\mid D}^{+}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Substituting \(\mathcal Q=H+\eta G\) gives
{\small
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda(A_j)
=
\max\Bigl\{
0,\,
&\frac12\theta_j^2(H_{jj}+\eta G_{jj})
\\
&-\frac12\theta_j^2
\Bigl[
(H_{Dj}+\eta G_{Dj})^{\mathsf T}
\\
&\qquad\qquad
(H_{DD}+\eta G_{DD})^+
(H_{Dj}+\eta G_{Dj})
\Bigr]
\Bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
}
\]
}

\phys{\(\Lambda(A_j)\) is the metric-regularized Schur nomination surrogate. It is low when deleting \(A_j\) has small frozen burden or when survivor coordinates recover a large fraction of that burden.}

\micro{The cost denominator turns the surrogate into a deletion ranking score.}

\deriv

\section*{Response-Only Damping Versus Full Recoverability}

\micro{The algebra changes depending on whether \(G\) damps only survivor motion or also measures recovery of the deleted tangent.}

\deriv

\theta_jH_{Dj},

\right\|^2 .

\end{aligned}

Thus
\[
(H_{DD}+\eta G_{DD})
\delta\theta_{D,\mathrm{full}}^\star
=
\theta_j(H_{Dj}+\eta G_{Dj}),
\]
\[
\boxed{
\delta\theta_{D,\mathrm{full}}^\star
=
\theta_j
(H_{DD}+\eta G_{DD})^+
(H_{Dj}+\eta G_{Dj}).
}
\]

\phys{The current implementation is the second route. The cross block \(G_{Dj}\) is not an optional decoration: it is the term that says the survivor response should geometrically reproduce the deleted tangent, not merely keep survivor motion small.}

\section*{Trust-Region Reading}

\micro{The metric term can be read as the Lagrange multiplier form of a geometric trust-region restriction.}

\deriv
Let the full deleted-plus-survivor metric residual be
\[
\mathcal R_j^G(\delta\theta_D)
=
\theta_j^2G_{jj}
-2\theta_jG_{Dj}^{\mathsf T}\delta\theta_D
+
\delta\theta_D^{\mathsf T}G_{DD}\delta\theta_D .
\]
A constrained local nomination problem is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta\theta_D^\star
&\in
\arg\min_{\delta\theta_D\in\mathbb R^d}
\Phi_{j,H}^\ominus(\delta\theta_D),
\\
&\text{subject to}
\qquad
\mathcal R_j^G(\delta\theta_D)\le \rho_\ominus^2 .
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(\eta\ge0\), the Lagrangian is
\[
\mathcal L_j^\ominus(\delta\theta_D,\eta)
=
\Phi_{j,H}^\ominus(\delta\theta_D)
+
\frac{\eta}{2}
\left(
\mathcal R_j^G(\delta\theta_D)-\rho_\ominus^2
\right).
\]
The KKT conditions are
\[
\nabla_{\delta\theta_D}\mathcal L_j^\ominus(\delta\theta_D,\eta)=0,
\]
\[
\mathcal R_j^G(\delta\theta_D)\le\rho_\ominus^2,
\qquad
\eta\ge0,
\]
\[
\eta\left(\mathcal R_j^G(\delta\theta_D)-\rho_\ominus^2\right)=0.
\]
The stationarity equation expands to
\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&=
-\theta_jH_{Dj}
+
H_{DD}\delta\theta_D
+
\eta(-\theta_jG_{Dj}+G_{DD}\delta\theta_D)
\\
&=
-\theta_j(H_{Dj}+\eta G_{Dj})
+
(H_{DD}+\eta G_{DD})\delta\theta_D .
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\delta\theta_D^\star(\eta)
=
\theta_j(H_{DD}+\eta G_{DD})^+
(H_{Dj}+\eta G_{Dj}).
\]

\phys{This trust-region reading is the most direct way to state the model: among local Schur responses, prefer responses that keep the deleted-plus-survivor tangent displacement geometrically recoverable.}

\micro{The response-only trust region gives a different KKT equation.}

so

\phys{This is not the implemented Paper-I metric route. It is a useful comparison because it shows exactly where the \(G_{Dj}\) cross block enters the current full recoverability route.}

\section*{Implementation Score Decomposition}

\micro{The code score is a completed-square Schur reduction written as frozen burden minus recovered credit.}

\deriv
With

\qquad
R=\mathcal Q_{DD}+\lambda I_d,
\qquad
\rhs=\theta_j\mathcal Q_{Dj},
\]
the code solves
\[
\delta=R^+\rhs.
\]
The code-level objective represented by \(R\) and \(\rhs\) is

\Phi_{j,R}^\ominus(\delta)
=
\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{jj}
-
\rhs^{\mathsf T}\delta
+
\frac12\delta^{\mathsf T}R\delta.
\]
Complete the square on the supported range of \(R\):
{\small
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_{j,R}^\ominus(\delta)
&=
\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{jj}
-
\frac12\rhs^{\mathsf T}R^+\rhs
\\
&\quad
+
\frac12
(\delta-R^+\rhs)^{\mathsf T}
R
(\delta-R^+\rhs),
\end{aligned}
\]
}
provided \(\rhs\) lies in the supported range of \(R\), with ridge/pseudoinverse conventions handling numerical support. At \(\delta=R^+\rhs\),
\[
\Phi_{j,R}^\ominus(\delta^\star)
=
\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{jj}
-
\frac12\rhs^{\mathsf T}R^+\rhs.

The same scalar is computed by

=
\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{jj},

=
\rhs^{\mathsf T}\delta
-
\frac12\delta^{\mathsf T}R\delta,

\[
\Lambda(A_j)
=
\max\{0,\mathrm{frozen}-\mathrm{credit}\}.
\]

\phys{The code does not need to explicitly write a Schur complement. It computes the same reduced scalar by solving the survivor response and subtracting the recovered credit from the frozen deleted-coordinate burden.}

\ne

\ne

\phys{The residual tells whether the survivor tangents geometrically reconstruct the deleted tangent. The score tells whether the metric-Schur local model thinks deletion is cheap enough, especially after dividing by the saved-cost denominator.}

\section*{Schur Complement As Elimination}

\micro{The Schur-complement expression is what remains after the survivor response has been optimized out of a block quadratic.}

\deriv
Let
\[
a\in\mathbb R,
\qquad
b\in\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
C\in\mathbb R^{d\times d},
\qquad
C=C^{\mathsf T}.
\]
For fixed \(\theta_j\in\mathbb R\) and free \(y\in\mathbb R^d\), define
\[
Q(y)
:=
\frac12
\begin{bmatrix}
-\theta_j\\
y
\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf T}
\begin{bmatrix}
a & b^{\mathsf T}\\
b & C
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
-\theta_j\\
y
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Expanding,
\[
Q(y)
=
\frac12\theta_j^2a
-
\theta_jb^{\mathsf T}y
+
\frac12y^{\mathsf T}Cy.
\]
The first-order condition is
\[
\nabla_yQ(y)
=
-\theta_jb+Cy=0,
\qquad
Cy^\star=\theta_jb.
\]
With pseudoinverse convention,
\[
y^\star=\theta_jC^+b.
\]
Substitution gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q(y^\star)
&=
\frac12\theta_j^2a
-
\theta_jb^{\mathsf T}(\theta_jC^+b)
+
\frac12(\theta_jC^+b)^{\mathsf T}C(\theta_jC^+b)
\\
&=
\frac12\theta_j^2a
-
\theta_j^2b^{\mathsf T}C^+b
+
\frac12\theta_j^2b^{\mathsf T}C^+CC^+b
\\
&=
\frac12\theta_j^2
\left(
a-b^{\mathsf T}C^+b
\right),
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(C^+CC^+ = C^+\) for the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse. The eliminated scalar is
\[
S_{a\mid C}^+
:=
a-b^{\mathsf T}C^+b.
\]

\phys{This is the generic Schur step. In prune notation, \(a\) is the deleted-coordinate frozen block, \(b\) is the deleted-survivor coupling, \(C\) is the survivor block, and \(y\) is the compensating survivor response.}

\micro{The implemented prune model is exactly this elimination pattern after substituting \(\mathcal Q=H+\eta G\).}

\deriv
Use the substitution
\[
a=\mathcal Q_{jj},
\qquad
b=\mathcal Q_{Dj},
\qquad
C=\mathcal Q_{DD},
\qquad
y=\delta\theta_D .
\]
Then
\[
Q(y)=\Phi_j^\ominus(\delta\theta_D),
\]
\[
\delta\theta_D^\star
=
\theta_j\mathcal Q_{DD}^+\mathcal Q_{Dj},
\]
\[
\Phi_j^\ominus(\delta\theta_D^\star)
=
\frac12\theta_j^2
\left(
\mathcal Q_{jj}
-
\mathcal Q_{Dj}^{\mathsf T}\mathcal Q_{DD}^+\mathcal Q_{Dj}
\right).
\]
With the explicit metric substitution,
\[
\mathcal Q_{jj}=H_{jj}+\eta G_{jj},
\]
\[
\mathcal Q_{Dj}=H_{Dj}+\eta G_{Dj},
\qquad
\mathcal Q_{DD}=H_{DD}+\eta G_{DD}.
\]
Therefore
{\small
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_j^\ominus(\delta\theta_D^\star)
&=
\frac12\theta_j^2
\left[
H_{jj}+\eta G_{jj}
-
(H_{Dj}+\eta G_{Dj})^{\mathsf T}
 \right.
\\
&\qquad\left.
(H_{DD}+\eta G_{DD})^+
(H_{Dj}+\eta G_{Dj})
\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
}

\phys{This is why the score has the form frozen burden minus Schur credit. The survivor coordinates were not ignored; they were optimized out of the local quadratic.}

\micro{The least-squares reading is exact only when the block being eliminated is a Gram block.}

\deriv
If
\[
C=T_D^{\mathsf T}T_D,
\qquad
b=T_D^{\mathsf T}y_j,
\qquad
a=y_j^{\mathsf T}y_j,
\]
then
\[
S_{a\mid C}^+
=
y_j^{\mathsf T}y_j
-
y_j^{\mathsf T}T_D
(T_D^{\mathsf T}T_D)^+
T_D^{\mathsf T}y_j.
\]
The least-squares residual is
\[
\min_{\beta\in\mathbb R^d}
\|y_j-T_D\beta\|^2
=
S_{a\mid C}^+.
\]
For the metric-only prune residual,
\[
y_j=\theta_j\Pi\tau_j,
\qquad
T_D\beta=\sum_{u\in D}\beta_u\Pi\tau_u,
\]
\[
C=G_{DD},
\qquad
b=\theta_jG_{Dj},
\qquad
a=\theta_j^2G_{jj}.
\]

\phys{The phrase ``least squares'' is literal for tangent recoverability under \(G\). The phrase ``Schur elimination'' is literal for the block quadratic. The implemented metric-Schur model combines these ideas by building \(\mathcal Q=H+\eta G\) before elimination.}

\section*{Stabilization Vocabulary}

\micro{The numerical linear algebra terms describe how the survivor block is solved when local tangent and curvature data are rank deficient or nearly flat.}

\deriv

\[

\qquad
\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal Q_{DD})\approx0,
\qquad
\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal Q_{DD})<0 .
\]
Horizontal tangent redundancy gives a concrete source:
\[
\sum_{u\in D}c_u\Pi\tau_u=0,
\qquad
c=(c_u)_{u\in D}\in\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
G_{DD}c=0 .
\]
Flat or saddle-like local energy geometry gives another:

c^{\mathsf T}H_{DD}c\approx0
\qquad
\text{or}
\qquad
c^{\mathsf T}H_{DD}c<0 .
\]
The ideal displayed solve is therefore
\[
\delta\theta_D^\star
=
\theta_j\mathcal Q_{DD}^{+}\mathcal Q_{Dj},
\]
while the implemented ridge-stabilized solve is
\[
\delta
=
(\mathcal Q_{DD}+\lambda I_d)^+(\theta_j\mathcal Q_{Dj}).
\]
If a positive-semidefinite projection is applied to a symmetric block, write
\[
\mathcal Q_{DD}
=
Q\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d)Q^{\mathsf T},
\]
\[
\mathcal Q_{DD}^{\mathrm{psd}}
:=
Q\operatorname{diag}(\max\{\alpha_1,0\},\ldots,\max\{\alpha_d,0\})Q^{\mathsf T}.
\]

\phys{The pseudoinverse solves on the supported range of the survivor block; the ridge solves a nearby damped problem; the PSD projection replaces an indefinite local block by a convexified surrogate. These are stabilization choices for nomination, not proofs that the nonlinear pruned branch will be good.}

\micro{The local terminology becomes compact once each score component is attached to its role.}

\deriv
The frozen local burden is
\[
B_j^{\mathrm{frozen}}
:=
\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{jj}.
\]
The recoverability credit is
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_j^{\mathrm{rec}}
&:=
\rhs^{\mathsf T}\delta
-
\frac12\delta^{\mathsf T}R\delta,
\\
R&=\mathcal Q_{DD}+\lambda I_d,
\qquad
\rhs=\theta_j\mathcal Q_{Dj}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The regularized Schur-reduced burden is
\[
\Lambda(A_j)
=
\max\{0,B_j^{\mathrm{frozen}}-C_j^{\mathrm{rec}}\}.
\]
The metric-only least-squares residual is
\[
\mathcal R_j^G(\delta)
=
\left\|
\theta_j\Pi\tau_j-\sum_{u\in D}\delta\theta_u\Pi\tau_u
\right\|^2.
\]
The measured nonlinear regression is
\[
\Delta E_j^{\mathrm{meas}}
=
E(U_k^{\ominus j},\vartheta_D^\star)
-
E(U_k,\boldsymbol\theta_k).
\]

\phys{\(B_j^{\mathrm{frozen}}\) is the local damage before compensation, \(C_j^{\mathrm{rec}}\) is the Schur recovery credited to survivor motion, \(\Lambda(A_j)\) is the nomination score, \(\mathcal R_j^G\) is geometry telemetry, and \(\Delta E_j^{\mathrm{meas}}\) is the delete/refit safety observable.}

\section*{Estimator-Query Accounting}

\micro{The Schur solve is classical; estimator queries enter only when the scalar entries used to build \(H\), \(G\), and the measured delete/refit energies are acquired.}

\deriv
Let \(B=\{j\}\cup D\) and \(m=|B|=d+1\). Write \(N_{\rm est}(X_B)\) for the number of scalar estimator primitives used to obtain the distinct entries of a symmetric block \(X_B\), after cache reuse. Then the nomination-stage query count is
\[
N_{\rm est}^{\rm nom}(B)
=
N_{\rm est}(H_B)+N_{\rm est}(G_B).
\]
Symmetry means that only entries with \(a\le b\) are acquired. If one bookkeeping unit is assigned to each distinct scalar entry, a previously uncached \(m\times m\) block contributes
\[
\begin{aligned}
N_{\rm est}(H_B)
&=N_{\rm est}(G_B)=\frac{m(m+1)}{2},
\\
N_{\rm est}^{\rm nom}(B)
&=m(m+1).
\end{aligned}
\]
This entry-count identity is a bookkeeping convention, not a claim that every entry requires the same number of circuits or shots. A backend decomposition may expand one scalar entry into several Pauli-term, parameter-shift, or overlap measurements; those lower-level costs belong in the estimator primitive attached to that entry.

When the same full \(n_k\times n_k\) blocks are reused to score every possible deletion, they are measured once:
\[
N_{\rm est}^{\rm all\ deletions}
=
N_{\rm est}(H)+N_{\rm est}(G),
\]
not \(n_k\) times this quantity. Forming \(\mathcal Q=H+\eta G\), extracting blocks, computing a pseudoinverse or ridge solve, evaluating the Schur credit, and ranking \(\Lambda(A_j)/K_j\) are classical operations and add zero estimator queries. Cached entries likewise add zero new queries.

The measured nonlinear delete/refit test is a separate stage. Its energy evaluations must be charged to the optimizer or validation routine that actually requests them; they are not hidden inside the Schur nomination count. Thus a transparent ledger separates
\[
N_{\rm est}^{\rm total}
=
N_{\rm est}^{\rm nom,new}
+
N_{\rm est}^{\rm delete/refit}
+
N_{\rm est}^{\rm validation},
\]
with cache hits and classical linear algebra recorded independently.

\phys{The important audit rule is that one deletion candidate does not receive a fresh charge for a matrix entry already measured for another candidate. Conversely, the nonlinear acceptance branch cannot be declared free merely because its candidate was nominated by a cached Schur block.}

\section*{Toy Examples}

\micro{The boundary case with no survivors shows the meaning of the frozen term.}

\deriv
If \(D=\varnothing\), then
\[
\mathcal Q_{DD}\in\mathbb R^{0\times0},
\qquad
\mathcal Q_{Dj}\in\mathbb R^0,
\qquad
\delta\theta_D^\star=0,
\]
\[
\Lambda(A_j)
=
\max\left\{
0,\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{jj}
\right\}
=
\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{jj}
\quad
(\mathcal Q_{jj}\ge0).
\]
\[
\mathcal R_j^G
=
\theta_j^2G_{jj}.
\]

\phys{With no survivor coordinates, the deleted generator cannot be locally recovered. The nomination score is just the frozen metric-Schur burden.}

\micro{The one-survivor case shows the Schur credit as a scalar explained-burden term.}

\deriv
Let \(D=\{\ell\}\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Q_{DD}&=[\mathcal Q_{\ell\ell}],
&
\mathcal Q_{Dj}&=[\mathcal Q_{\ell j}],
\\
R&=[\mathcal Q_{\ell\ell}+\lambda],
&
\rhs&=[\theta_j\mathcal Q_{\ell j}].
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(\mathcal Q_{\ell\ell}+\lambda\ne0\),
\[
\delta\theta_\ell^\star
=
\frac{\theta_j\mathcal Q_{\ell j}}{\mathcal Q_{\ell\ell}+\lambda}.
\]
The ridge-stabilized credit is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{credit}
&=
\theta_j\mathcal Q_{\ell j}\delta\theta_\ell^\star
-\frac12(\mathcal Q_{\ell\ell}+\lambda)(\delta\theta_\ell^\star)^2
\\
&=
\frac12
\frac{\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{\ell j}^2}{\mathcal Q_{\ell\ell}+\lambda}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the score is
\[
\Lambda(A_j)
=
\max\left\{
0,\,
\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{jj}
-\frac12
\frac{\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{\ell j}^2}{\mathcal Q_{\ell\ell}+\lambda}
\right\}.
\]
With displayed ridge suppressed,
\[
\Lambda(A_j)
=
\max\left\{
0,\,
\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{jj}
-\frac12\theta_j^2\mathcal Q_{\ell j}^2\mathcal Q_{\ell\ell}^{+}
\right\}.
\]

\phys{The deletion is attractive when the survivor direction strongly couples to the removed direction relative to the ridge-stabilized survivor stiffness \(\mathcal Q_{\ell\ell}+\lambda\).}

\micro{The zero-metric case recovers the ordinary Hessian Schur nomination surrogate.}

\deriv
If \(\eta=0\), then
\[
\mathcal Q=H,
\qquad
\delta\theta_D^\star
=
\theta_jH_{DD}^{+}H_{Dj},
\]
\[
\Lambda(A_j)
=
\max\left\{
0,\,
\frac12\theta_j^2H_{jj}
-\frac12\theta_j^2H_{Dj}^{\mathsf T}H_{DD}^{+}H_{Dj}
\right\}.
\]

\phys{The metric route reduces to a pure Hessian Schur deletion surrogate when Fubini--Study weighting is disabled.}
