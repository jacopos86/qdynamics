## Executive decision

The most defensible future experiment is not another independently tuned
FM-SNAKE route. It is a matched wrapper around the then-current successful
Paper-I route:

1. preserve the Paper-I selector, candidate pool, admission policy, accepted
   refit, stopping rule, and scientific settings;
2. add a branch-local FM outer-information state that initially operates only
   in shadow mode;
3. distinguish exact same-ray frame extension, predictive inter-state
   transport after nonlinear refit, and measured endpoint registration;
4. let Procrustes act only at a measured reanchor with measured cross-state
   tangent information;
5. activate measurement omission only after shadow predictions, closure

The Paper-I algorithm remains the decision authority. FM is first a geometric
predictor and measurement-cache layer around its outer loop. Route names are
secondary to this behavioral contract.

## Non-negotiable conceptual separation

Three operations were repeatedly conflated and must remain separate.

### 1. Same-ray structural extension

After a singleton or batch is admitted at exactly zero amplitude, the physical
ray has not moved. The inherited tangent frame and old--old Gram block therefore
carry forward by exact inclusion. Only the admitted direction's missing
candidate--old and candidate--candidate geometry must be supplied. Winning
candidate blocks already acquired by Phase II or Phase III should be consumed
from their typed cache rather than remeasured.

This is exact growth reuse, not transport.

### 2. Inter-state prediction after nonlinear refit

The accepted full-ansatz refit moves the physical ray. The endpoint tangent
space can have different stretch, support, and ambient orientation. A predicted
metric or connection model may propagate the old supported chart, curvature,
and secant information, but its result is a labeled prediction. Endpoint Gram
matrices alone do not determine the cross-state orientation of tangent frames.

This is the actual outer-iteration transport problem.

### 3. Measured endpoint registration and reanchoring

When endpoint geometry and cross-state tangent overlaps are measured, the two
whitened frames can be registered by a classical polar/Procrustes calculation.
Procrustes removes whitening gauge rotation and aligns corresponding resolved
modes. The overlap supplied to it is new quantum information unless an
equivalent primitive has already been acquired.

This is an authoritative anchor operation, not a measurement-saving transport
law.

### Causal interpretation

The active implementation did not demonstrate broad net measurement reduction:

- only the inherited old--old block was eligible for prediction;
- candidate--active and candidate--candidate geometry still had to be acquired;
- exact controls already deduplicated substantial shared work;
- frequent exact reanchors erased much of the predicted metric avoidance;
- completed ledgers recorded `closure_probe_primitives_acquired = 0`, so the
  intended cheap low-rank closure/correction layer was not actually realized;
- some completed paths reported uncertified transport, no applied outer
  curvature transport, and zero outer secant updates;
- terminal `S_alg` and accuracy differences also included downstream trajectory
  divergence and therefore were not fixed-prefix estimates of reuse alone.

The strong--weak matched pair is an especially useful warning: despite 872
directly recorded old-block entries avoided, the active row used 2,876 more
unique logical primitives than its exact-current control. Extra refit work and
fallbacks outweighed nominal old-block avoidance.

This evidence diagnoses the attempted implementation. It does not mathematically
disprove outer-information reuse.

## Failure record future agents must not repeat

1. **Route perturbation drift.** Selector policies, batching, novelty, beam,
   pruning, optimizer identity, coordinate chart, and FM ownership changed in
   overlapping experiments. A future reuse test must change only outer
   information authority.
2. **Procrustes misuse.** Procrustes was sometimes discussed as if it generated
   measurement-free transport. It only registers frames after the required
   cross-state data exist.
3. **Immediate exact fallback.** The implementation often jumped from an
   untrusted prediction directly to a full exact anchor instead of using the
   proposed directional or low-rank closure probes and minimum-change
   correction.
4. **Unsupported-gradient projection.** A condition such as
   `raw_metric_null_gradient_incompatible` invalidates the propagated geometry
   hypothesis. It must not be hidden by projecting away the incompatible
   gradient or by changing whitening thresholds.
5. **Structural rollback.** Geometric invalidation must never undo an accepted
   Paper-I structural transition. Structural rollback is not part of this
   future outer-reuse aim.
6. **Accounting conflation.** Receipt reuse, predictive avoidance, exact
   fallback, raw matrix-entry counts, unique estimator primitives, circuits,
   and shots are distinct currencies.
7. **Trajectory attribution.** Once the selected operator sequence diverges,
   terminal energy, Qiskit cost, `nfev`, and aggregate `S_alg` no longer isolate
   the reuse mechanism.
8. **Unbounded checkpoint state.** The final July 21--22 continuation spent
   roughly 13 hours deserializing/rebuilding an enormous estimator-call ledger
   before completing a new scientific round. Resume state must use bounded,
   indexed, streaming, or summarized accounting rather than replaying a giant
   occurrence history into memory.
9. **Success by tests alone.** Focused tests establish contracts and numerical
   identities; they do not establish transport accuracy or measurement savings
   on the scientific route.

## Recommended future research protocol

### Stage A: resolve and freeze the successful Paper-I baseline

Resolve the then-current visible Paper-I result to its exact source artifact and
manifest. Freeze its controller, pool, admission policy, novelty behavior,
beam/prune/batch settings, accepted refit, coordinate chart, seed, stopping
rule, and estimator-accounting convention. Do not construct a baseline from
current defaults or a remembered route label.

### Stage B: shadow prediction with exact authority

Run the baseline unchanged. At every outer transition, compute the FM predicted
old-block metric, supported frame, transport, curvature action, and hypothetical
primitive avoidance, but let exact Paper-I geometry determine every selection
and refit. The shadow must not alter operators, parameters, stopping, or
structural state.

Record at least:

- supported ranks and retained spectral gaps;
- predicted-versus-exact old-block Gram error;
- principal-angle or registered-frame orientation error on common support;
- predicted-versus-exact gradient and Hessian actions on traversed and sentinel
  directions;
- closure residual by probe family;
- predicted, corrected, reanchored, invalidated, and never-eligible entries;
- hypothetical net unique primitives avoided after charging closure probes and
  reanchors;
- checkpoint size and resume/deserialization wall time.

Energy trust and geometry confidence are different diagnostics. Predicted versus
realized energy reduction tests the local objective model; it does not by itself
certify the transported frame.

### Stage C: fixed-prefix active-reuse control

Before allowing FM to influence online selection, replay a fixed accepted
operator prefix from the Paper-I baseline. Permit active omission only for
old-block primitives whose predictions passed the shadow criteria. Continue to
acquire candidate-specific blocks. This isolates measurement reuse from
trajectory divergence.

Compare exact and reuse executions at identical states and prefixes. The key
quantity is

```text
net unique primitives avoided
  = eligible exact primitives omitted
  - closure-probe primitives added
  - reanchor/fallback primitives added.
```

Do not infer this value from raw matrix dimensions or optimizer `nfev`.

### Stage D: online active reuse

Only after the fixed-prefix control passes should predicted outer geometry be
allowed to influence the live controller. Invalid transport must fall back to
the exact Paper-I decision without structural rollback. Every divergence from
the control must be attributable to an explicitly validated information change,
not to a hidden route/profile change.

## Candidate dominant construction

The outer-transport pedagogical volume selects an anchored split construction:

1. exact Gram--Schur extension at same-ray growth;
2. a positive metric-strain predictor for intrinsic ruler evolution;
3. a separate skew/orientation connection predictor;
4. low-rank deterministic closure using newly observed next-round geometry;
5. minimum-change correction on identified defect subspaces;
6. event-driven exact Gram and cross-frame reanchoring;
7. Procrustes registration only at those measured anchors;
8. outer curvature/secant recycling only in a certified common target frame.

This remains the leading mathematical proposal. It is not yet established by
the completed implementation, because the cheap closure/correction hierarchy
was not exercised and transport was frequently replaced by exact fallback.

## Definition of a successful future result

The first meaningful result is not a lower terminal energy from a different
trajectory. It is a matched, fixed-prefix demonstration that:

- the successful Paper-I scientific route is unchanged;
- shadow predictions accurately reproduce exact reusable geometry on certified
  support;
- active reuse omits only primitives justified by that certificate;
- closure probes and reanchors are fully charged;
- net unique logical estimator work is lower at the same states, prefix, and
  accuracy;
- checkpoint/resume remains bounded and practical;
- all invalidation events preserve accepted Paper-I structure and fall back to
  exact authority.

Only after that result exists does it become scientifically meaningful to test
whether active online outer reuse also preserves or improves the Paper-I
error--cost trajectory.
