## Official scope

Physical Review A covers important developments in AMO physics, quantum science, and related fundamental concepts. Quantum algorithms and simulation methods fit when the physics/QIS contribution is clear.

## Article types and formatting expectations

Regular Articles have no length limit. Letters have a 4,500-word limit; Perspectives are generally invitation-only; Comments/Replies are 3,500 words. APS/REVTeX style and data-availability policy apply.

## Abstract style

Abstracts should be concise, unstructured, and physics-facing. State method and demonstrated benchmark scope without flagship-level hype.

## Introduction style

Introduction should move quickly from physical/algorithmic problem to precise method gap. Less broad QIST marketing than PRX Quantum.

## Related-work density

Moderate. Cite direct method families and model/physics context; avoid exhaustive literature unless needed for scope.

## Methods/results balance

Methods can be detailed; results should be physically interpretable and baseline-aware. PRA tolerates careful algorithmic method papers.

## Equation tolerance

High enough for algorithmic equations, but keep derivations organized and physically motivated.

## Figure/table conventions

Use standard APS figure/table style. Prefer comparator tables and clearly labeled error/resource plots.

## Data/code availability expectations

APS data availability statement required; public data/code encouraged and nonpublic data must be justified and available privately on reasonable request.

## Cover-letter or submission guidance

APS cover-letter guidance applies. Letters must justify priority/short format; regular articles can be full method papers.

## Why Paper I fits / does not fit

Paper I fits as a quantum-algorithm/static ansatz-construction method if positioned around quantum simulation rather than materials-specific claims. It does not fit if results are only internal HH engineering.

## Why Paper II fits / does not fit

Paper II fits well as variational quantum dynamics with McLachlan projection and resource-aware comparisons. It does not fit if exact-assisted diagnostics are mistaken for QPU-faithful method claims.

## Evidence threshold that would make this journal realistic

Exact baselines, method equations, resource/error plots, direct ADAPT or dynamics comparators, conservative claims.

## Claims too strong for this journal without more evidence

Quantum advantage, hardware readiness, or superiority over all ADAPT/dynamics methods without matched evidence.

## Reusable drafting guidance

- Use APS concise technical style.
- Put physical or algorithmic significance before implementation detail.
- Use exact comparator names.
- Keep limitations explicit but not apologetic.
