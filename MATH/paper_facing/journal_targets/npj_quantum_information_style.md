## Official scope

npj Quantum Information considers all aspects of quantum information research, including quantum computing, quantum machine learning, communication, cryptography, sensing, devices, and foundational quantum information science.

## Article types and formatting expectations

npj Series journals are online-only open access and impose no strict overall word/page limits, but Article guidelines include title up to 15 words, abstract up to 150 words, unheaded Introduction, headed Results, unheaded Discussion, headed Methods, mandatory Data availability, Code availability where applicable, mandatory author contributions/competing interests, and references around 60 as guidance.

## Abstract style

Abstract should be unstructured, short, and result-forward. Avoid dense notation. State the bottleneck, method, and demonstrated resource/accuracy outcome.

## Introduction style

Introduction should read like Nature Portfolio: broad enough for quantum-information readers, quickly narrowing to the gap and method. Do not overuse citations in every sentence.

## Related-work density

Selective. Cite key prior methods and closest overlaps; avoid bibliography padding. Reference limit guidance encourages discipline.

## Methods/results balance

Results-first structure is expected: present main empirical/method result before a detailed Methods section. Methods can be detailed but should not replace result narrative.

## Equation tolerance

Moderate. Equations are acceptable, but main text should not become a derivation monograph. Put derivations in Methods/SI.

## Figure/table conventions

Figures should be polished multi-panel summaries. Legends can be long but should explain panels clearly. Tables should be compact and high-value.

## Data/code availability expectations

Data availability is mandatory. Code availability is required where applicable; central custom code/algorithms must be available to editors/reviewers and should be released in a DOI-minting repository when possible.

## Cover-letter or submission guidance

A cover letter, editable manuscript, figures, SI/checklists are expected. Related manuscripts must be disclosed.

## Why Paper I fits / does not fit

Paper I fits if the method is made useful to the quantum-information/resource-reduction community and evidence is figure-led. It does not fit if it reads as a long internal ADAPT derivation with limited external benchmark framing.

## Why Paper II fits / does not fit

Paper II fits if the trajectory/resource Pareto and measurement-compatible controller story are clear and near-term relevant. It does not fit if it is too model-specific or too exact-simulation-heavy.

## Evidence threshold that would make this journal realistic

Strong visual evidence, reproducible code/data, concise claim boundaries, direct modern comparators, and a clear data/code availability plan.

## Claims too strong for this journal without more evidence

Overbroad "practical advantage," hidden code/data, unsupported hardware-readiness, or manuscript structures that bury the Results behind long derivations.

## Reusable drafting guidance

- Draft with Results before exhaustive Methods.
- Keep titles under 15 words if targeting npj style.
- Use a short unstructured abstract.
- Prepare data/code availability from the start.
- Move derivation detail to Methods or SI.
