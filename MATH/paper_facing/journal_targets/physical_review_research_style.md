## Official scope

Physical Review Research is a broad fully open-access Physical Review journal covering all areas of physics and related fields, including methodological and interdisciplinary work.

## Article types and formatting expectations

Regular Articles have no length limit. Letters have a 4,500-word limit. Perspectives are invited/solicited or presubmission-proposed. Comments/Replies are 3,500 words. APS style and data-availability rules apply.

## Abstract style

Abstract should be accessible across physics subfields and state the advance, method object, and evidence scope.

## Introduction style

Introduction can be broader than PRA/PRB, but should quickly identify why the method matters beyond one implementation.

## Related-work density

Moderate-to-high. Since PRR is broad, contextualize against both algorithmic and physical simulation literatures.

## Methods/results balance

Allows fuller method/result packages than a Letter. Good venue for complete benchmark matrices and ablations.

## Equation tolerance

High; appendices/supplement can carry derivations. Main text should preserve narrative clarity.

## Figure/table conventions

Figures and tables should make the result portable: method schematic, benchmark matrix, Pareto fronts, ablations, limitations.

## Data/code availability expectations

APS data availability statement required; data/code sharing encouraged under the Physical Review policy.

## Cover-letter or submission guidance

APS cover-letter guidance applies, including explicit handling of joint submissions/transfers if relevant.

## Why Paper I fits / does not fit

Paper I fits if framed as a broad physics-methods contribution for variational quantum simulation. It does not fit if novelty is too narrow or benchmark evidence too local.

## Why Paper II fits / does not fit

Paper II fits as a broad variational-dynamics method with benchmark evidence and transparent decision-data separation.

## Evidence threshold that would make this journal realistic

Complete evidence package, honest scope, cross-family tests, and clear distinction from closest prior methods.

## Claims too strong for this journal without more evidence

Flagship-level impact claims without PRX-level evidence; practical hardware advantage without noise/shot support.

## Reusable drafting guidance

- Favor complete, transparent evidence over hype.
- Make the method schematic self-contained.
- Use limitations as scientific boundaries.
- Keep exact diagnostic data separate from controller decisions.
